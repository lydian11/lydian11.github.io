# Solving Galactic Puzzle Hunt (2025)

This post contains unmarked spoilers for Galactic Puzzlehunt 2025.

While [Penchant](https://penchantpuzzlehunt.com/) was absolutely thrilling and exceeded my expectations tenfold! Galactic Puzzle Hunt was the big hunt I was looking forward to after coming back from my puzzle burnout. It all lined up very nicely too, with the hunt beginning on the day I had my last exam for the semester at my University! Galactic is easily one of my favorite recurring hunts, if not my all-time favorite, so I was really excited to see what they'd do this year! I signed up with the Blob Wizards' competitive team, and patiently waited until 19:28 BRT!

Upon opening the puzzles we were met with this year's gimmick, puzzles would come in pieces and it was our job to assemble them together! I was very excited to see how this would be used throughout the hunt. We also noticed the 🌶️🍌🟢💙 system, which I thought that would be a very reasonable element on this hunt where, lest you'd potentially let solvers spend hours struggling to crack a puzzle only to find out it wasn't solvable in the first place, so I really think this was a great decision for this year's gimmick!

This post will divide its sections by rounds in their intended order, not necessarily the chronological order we solved puzzles at, in an attempt to keep everything tidy and not jump much from round to round.

## [Pan's Laboratory](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/pans_laboratory)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">This round had the more "vanilla" interpretation of the gimmick which was a great way to ease us into the idea of the hunt-wide gimmick. At this point, though, it never once crossed my mind that the pieces would be interpreted differently in different rounds!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Four Pieces</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">This was the first puzzle we looked at and solved! We were quick enough to notice the common bigrams, and realized relatively fast that these were four corner US states, and from there it took a bit to figure out how to read the extraction, but it all went very smoothly! I really appreciated how this puzzle used the spatial orientation of the states to play with the pieces and it seemed like the perfect introductory puzzle for the hunt's gimmick, and I was surprised to find out later it was a late replacement as it felt very deliberate!</div>


<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Ring Cycle</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">After Four Pieces, I went to look at this puzzle alongside some teammates, it was trickier than I thought it'd be to get everything to match, but eventually we got it! We spent a lot of time just trying to parse the cluephrase, before getting the Noble Eightfold Path aha and solving the puzzle, yet another great example of the piece gimmick being used!</div>


<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>One-Time Use</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">For this puzzle, we were trying to make it work as an MTG-only puzzle, but eventually the aha that this included other card games and we were looking for keywords synonymous with MTG's "exile". After that I found the ordering really quick of the ‰ part without the last piece, which I think made this our first non-💙 solve if I'm not mistaken. It was a pretty fun solve, and while relatively simple I appreciated the solve path of the ‰ aspect! Great introductory-round puzzle!</div><br>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">After that and some work by my teammates on the other puzzles that had too many words for my squirrel brain to focus on, it was getting late and I thought I'd keep the reasonable sleep schedule I was having before the hunt came along, especially as this hunt seemed like it would take a while considering the number of puzzles and our relative progress for the first few hours!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>square</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">I woke up in the morning to this completely logicked out but not extracted. My teammates that worked on this were kinda zeroed in on extracting from the loop and not the clues on the outside, for a moment I almost discarded the correct idea of extracting from the clues due to the 00000 before reading off, but thankfully I had said my idea in our chat before considering discarding it, and my teammates read out LEOPOLD by treating the 00000 as nothing. I guess that works, but it definitely threw me off a bit!</div>


<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Chains</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">We had no idea where to start on this one, until after hours of staring Rusty popped in to suggest these could be island chains. I ended up doing a reasonable portion of the work of identifying them, but things became a bit easier when someone pointed out the piece separations were the equator and the international date line, and that, by virtue of that these were mostly all in the pacific, I had no clue on the extraction but ffao got that pretty quickly!</div>


<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>[META] Surviving the Open World</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">After folks got <i>Unfamiliar Characters</i> shortly after the <i>Chains</i> solve, we had the meta unlocked. And this was the first time we got like, actually stuck... Hours and hours of theories of trying to map the different regions to different survival games (and of course we mentioned Minecraft several times during that process), trying to mess with the rotation of the pieces as the meta pieces were given rotated (still don't understand what that's about), trying to think of all weird ways to assign letters to pieces, we eventually had to wait patiently until it was 19:28 again and ask for a hint. It's a very common experience, I think, in puzzlehunts to feel very stupid once your hint arrives, I think this case might've been up there as one of the most stupid I've felt upon reading a hint, haha! For someone that grew up with this game, I think I should have been able to reach the intended aha on my own, but it just never came up even after hours and hours of staring. It's not very signposted by the puzzle, but it feels like the only thing it could've been after it clicks together. As soon as the hint request told us about a survival game with a 3x3 grid, I knew exactly what to do and we got the solve down pretty quickly! All in all, I really liked this meta! I think for a R1 meta it feels a <i>bit</i> under-signposted, maybe a stronger nudge in title or flavor text would've been welcome, but it's such a cool concept especially alongside the piece gimmick that I can't help but be a fan!</div><br>

Before we move on to the next round, with this meta this is also the point where we found out the hint system this year is a bit different. GPH offered the option to ask for a small nudge, a "sledgehammer" hint that just gives you everything you need, or a flexible option that lets the hinting team answer with their best judgement. I think this is a brilliant idea, and I hope to see more hunts use this system in the future! I generally will always prefer a nudgier hint so I can preserve the most I can of the aha generation, but sometimes you're just done with the puzzle and you just want to move on, so I really value the system and how it cuts down on having to explicitly ask in text for a stronger/weaker hint, or require a chain of follow-ups to a powerful hint!

## [Abandoned Site](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/abandoned_site)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">We opened the new round to discovered that the puzzles were spread out across multiple pieces, I was curious how this gimmick would work for this round, and very eager to get to experience more of the hunt soon, but that's not quite how things worked out.. This round is when we got stuck big-time! I'd thought the 9 hours between solves until <i>Pan's Laboratory</i>'s meta was bad, but it ended up taking us another 25 to solve another puzzle. We only had two 💙s at the time, and both had us unable to progress much, we just weren't sure if the answer bank was part of the same puzzle as the seven subpuzzles we were seeing for <i>Seven Puzzles</i> so we struggled to make much progress, and for <i>Polygons</i> we did make great progress early on but we were unable to finish out the solve, we ended up hinting both of them! Which meant we had to wait until Sunday evening to progress, and the whole time we were just staring at things and trying to make them work. Thankfully we persevered and I did end up enjoying this round quite a lot!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Seven Puzzles</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">After getting the hint that the answer list was, indeed, part of this puzzle, things started to fall into place very quickly! In hindsight I think that was our bad because it does seem rather intuitive that the seven puzzles from <i>Seven Puzzles</i> which are unsolvable would go along with the big answer list that we had already separated into a few smaller groups, but it is what it is! Nugget Bridge was the first minimeta we solved, and I was very disappointed for not having contributed to the Pokémon content in this hunt, my teammates also beat me to identifying the answers as Pokémon. The only minimeta I got on my own was the hashi one as the very last we solved, when by elimination we knew which answers it had to have, and I got the aha from the letter patterns, very cool ideas throughout in the minimetas, but this one was especially my favorite! As for the metameta, it became clear quickly to us that each answer related to a bridge, but we were a bit unsure how to use the German words. I noticed what seemed to be a lot of repeating letters in the answers and that led me down a path of trying to place each letter in a landmass and bridge from the bridge starting positions but that broke very quickly. I then considered there were 4 German words and 4 landmasses and that we should probably associate those somehow, with the extraction mechanism that would eventually be the correct in mind. It was then that ffao pointed out that they all begin with the same letters, and we got extracting! And after 25 hours we finally had another solve! And yet, despite all the time it took this ended up being my favorite feeder in the whole hunt. The thematic link of the bridge themed subpuzzles with an absolutely brilliant metameta just really wowed me here. For the second time we'd been stuck on a puzzle for a while, but after hinting it, it felt like it was the only thing it could've been!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Polygons</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">This was the puzzle we'd gotten the most work done on upon the round unlock after solving <i>Pan's Laboratory</i>'s meta. It was getting late-ish for me so I just dropped in pretty early to suggest that we might use a cuboctahedron and went to bed, waking up to find a lot of work done, and an ordering of A–L extracted. In the meantime ffao had suggested the anticuboctahedron as an alternate polyhedron we could make, and while folks had tried to make that work overnight, I was so sure it had to be the right thing and we were hardstuck on everything else that I tried it too on Krita for redundancy, but it didn't work. I even considered an anticuboctahedron but with all the conditions being false instead of true, but looking more closely at them, most of the conditions resulted in a lot more false than true edges, so I just didn't bother trying as my intuition told me it would've been very non-unique. We also needed to hint this one, and we did it shortly after Seven Puzzles, finding out that we needed to use two smaller polyhedra instead of another big one. It clicked pretty fast what we were supposed to do after that and we solved the puzzle shortly thereafter. In hindsight I guess it's less clear how to get an antipode on the anticuboctahedron, but in my mind we'd inscribe the shape on a sphere and trace a line to the other side, but I guess doing it with a cube and an octahedron also makes sense. It was just something that never occured to us to consider splitting the polygons into two separate polyhedra, but it seems this pitfall wasn't very common, so I think the puzzle is probably fine! Ultimately I really liked the idea here, especially working with 3D geometry makes it really fun, although this one felt a bit less satisfying of a solve-post-hint than the first meta and Seven Puzzles just because the anticuboctahedron aha felt so true until we were told it wasn't it...</div><br>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">As I'd gone past midnight working on the two puzzles we'd hinted I went to bed. I woke up the next morning to a huge amount of progress, surely making up for most of the dearth of solves for most of the weekend! All feeders on the Abandoned Site and the meta were solved! And we had two new rounds unlocked!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>[META] The Web</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">While I was asleep the whole time, I still want to talk about this meta, as it's one of my favorite ideas in the hunt! Of course, it's harder to have a well-formed opinion on a meta you didn't solve, but the gimmick of using the pieces to contain multiple parts of the puzzle, and have that make a meta-relevant web using webdings and the pieces' emoji names is such a fun concept! It explored the pieces on a very different way than the what we saw on <i>Pan's Laboratory</i>, but it still felt faithful to the piece mechanic and definitely a fun idea! The only thing I'd say is that I had no clue what webdings was and I definitely would've gotten stuck in that part if I were solving alone. I don't know how many teams got stuck in that part but it seems like something rather hard to break through on if you haven't heard of it before on a meta of all things! Still, I'm really happy this meta exists, I think it's the mark of a good meta when you slept through it and after being explained how it works go "damn, I wish I were around for that" and that's definitely what happened here!</div>

## [(A)isle of Crosswords](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/aisle_of_crosswords)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">After I woke up to both opened rounds and some work having begun in <i>(A)isle of Crosswords</i> already, alongside my intimidation from that round's gimmick, I decided to look at The Crater instead. Still, with the crossword powerhouses on my team, especially Autumn Leaves, it didn't take too long for the feeders to fall while I was looking elsewhere!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>[META] (A)isle of Crosswords</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">I did jump in later to try to make sense of the meta though, and by the time we got this meta it was already Tuesday in my timezone. This was also a meta we ended up having to hint, as, unfortunately, we got too rabbit holed on reading the diagram as 1D backwards, 1A, 2D in order to make a continuous path (like ↑ → ↓), instead of actually reading the crossword entries in order 1A, 1D, 2D. Now that I explain written out or thought process, it does make plenty of sense to do it the way it was done in the hunt, much more than how we did it in my opinion, but it just didn't click for us. I think the biggest problem is that it's a bit hard to verify if the reading is wrong or right with how this meta works, it would always be possible for there to be another parsing of the ?-type clues that could've resolved to something sensible, I think, so that made it a bit tricky to leave the rabbit hole on our own? I'm not sure... In general, I really like the idea of this round, even though I got to work on pretty much zero of it, it seemed like the most interesting use of the pieces to me, and looking back I wish I got a bit more involved with it instead of wimping away to look at The Crater instead, but the puzzles did seem a bit daunting, and I'm not the strongest crossword solver in the first place, so maybe I did what's best for myself! Still, the meta ended up leaving a slight sour taste on my mouth when we solved it, as it felt a bit too loose, and like there wasn't a huge aha outside of the answers being ?-type crossword clues, but after a few days I think I've come around to it and I think I can appreciate it a bit more!</div>

## [The Crater](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/the_crater)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">As previously stated, on monday morning I woke up to both <i>(A)isle of Crosswords</i> and this opened, with some work having begun in <i>(A)isle of Crosswords</i> already, alongside my intimidation from that round's gimmick, I decided to look at this round instead!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>A Cryptic Has Arrived!</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">While gravitating to the cryptic definitely wouldn't be my first instinct, my other options didn't seem initially very welcoming to my skill set either. Dream Team seemed to be about LOL which I thought it would be better to leave to teammates, and I do like cryptics in spite of being scared of them! After someone suggested the Japanese onomatopoeia gimmick, I actually solved a few cryptic clues which is way more than I thought I'd do after initially joining the group for the solve here. But oddly we noticed some very curious clues that didn't seem to use that mechanic and resolved to some rather strange words and phrases. We then realized these were Pikmin ship parts and were for the longest time trying to work out how that tied into the japanese onomatopoeia part of the puzzle. I eventually went look at something else. This was solved and I came back to find out that the Pikmin parts went completely unused. My mind then went to "oh this is probably meta relevant then", but it was a bit tricky to work with the puzzle trying to work the pieces into the solve path.</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Convergence</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">We were having a bit of a hard time interpreting which rules were for the smaller pentominoes and which ones were for the 8×5 rectangle. No one in our team knew of any polyomino solvers we could use for this at the time, so I decided to try to code my own, ignoring any of the extra rules first, just to get an idea of what solutions there could be. Turns out I may not be so good at coding, because I didn't do anything for counting symmetric solutions as the same and yet I got 14 solutions when, if I'm not mistaken, the outcome probably should be a multiple of four, so my code was very likely wrong, and unfortunately I wasn't able to debug my way out of this one, so we got stuck again. In the end, I ended up backsolving this one, which is a bit of a shame, as I really liked the idea here!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>Identify... INDEX, Solve</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">I didn't help too much on this one, other than identifying the `(demisemi|semi)?(hemidemisemi)*quaver`. I was mostly kinda sleepy by the time my team unlocked and were making progress on this one, so I was just looking at things being solved and nodding along without actually contributing much, as my brain was absolutely fried at this point! But I still wanted to comment on it because it feels like such a neat idea and it was executed so nicely! This feels like the kind of puzzle someone would've written a decade ago and it would've been a classic!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>[META] Picking Up the Pieces</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">So this one is a bit embarrassing, we had the right idea, but for some reason we made a transcription error and we had the pieces on <i>A Cryptic Has Arrived!</i> all associated with the wrong letters. Since the right idea wasn't working we got kinda carried away with hallucinations. One of them included me counting 26 redstone torches in the art map, and trying to find how that could possibly make sense with this meta! After having to use a hint at Tuesday evening to error-correct that, i randomgrammed the letters we did have to make our answer. It had to be explained to me that the reading order was clockwise (due to there not being a canonical north on the Pikmin minimaps) for me to finally be able to see it, so I was very confused to what was going on until then, but after things fell into place the meta made a lot more sense! I still wish the ship pieces could've been hidden in a more interesting way in A Cryptic Has Arrived!, I thought they were too on the nose and kind of gave the whole thing away, I think this round could've been a bit more interesting if we solved the puzzle without ever noticing them and then went to look back and spotted them! I don't have much of a suggestion on how that'd go, but as is, this meta ended up feeling a bit aha-less for me as well, on top of the piece usage not being as interesting as the other puzzles. That said, I did love the choice of a Pikmin dataset being used here!</div>

## [The Atoll](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/the_atoll)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">On Wednesday I woke up to <i>The Atoll</i> being unlocked, <i>Reduce</i> being solved and <i>Spooflantu Cultural Exchange</i> was most of the way there. Before we unlocked the meta here, I was pretty sure that the 12 atolls would clue an A-to-L gimmick here alongside the round's title name, but what we got as much, much better! In general, this ended up being a bit of a slower round for me in the feeder department, I was around the whole day but I ended up just watching my teammates solve for the most part struggling to contribute too much to the feeders, I don't know if it was the exhaustion getting to me, or if it was my room for growth as a solver still showing a bit, but yeah, things just weren't quite clicking for me that day, I mostly watched <i>Mark of Identification</i>, which I think is a really cute puzzle!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>[META] The Atoll</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">So I wake up Thursday morning to this being unlocked, and I immediately get an adrenaline boost when I get told it's a music puzzle! The association of feeder answers to types of chords is so clever and fresh, and I found the parallel between the atolls and the circle of fifths very cute! I ended up retreading work done overnight as I didn't spot it in our spreadsheet (oops!) realizing independently that you could make chords with the given pieces and "logic" out what other notes were part of that chord depending on the root, and tried several ideas on extraction, but nothing was clicking. Funnily enough, I had ideas that were very similar to what was actually used in the puzzle, except instead of using the notation in semitones like the puzzle does, e.g. a fifth is +7, I was using the interval notation, like a fifth is well, a fifth! It was then that a teammate showed up to explain the work they'd done overnight, with the extra info that the puzzle pieces corresponded to the notes in ascending order, which made placing everything together a lot easier, we were just missing the final extraction. That teammate also realized that longer feeders seemed to correspond to chords with higher extensions (in particular that it was close to a 3:1 ratio between maximum interval and letters), and I immediately knew what to do from there. I shared the idea and someone beat me to extraction while I tried to make everything pretty and clean in our sheet. I still wanted to finish my job though, so I continued "solving" even after we'd answered correctly just for the neatness of it. I mean at this point we weren't really speedrunning anyway, were we? Immediately after I got that aha though, I knew that this meta was my all-time favorite I've done so far, and that opinion hasn't changed since! It's such an elegant construction, with the feeder association, the circle of fifths, the chord assembly, and the letters matching with the intervals in semitone notation. It just, pun intended, hit all the right notes for me. Which is probably what you'd expect from someone who chose the online username of <i>lydian</i> for themself! I also backsolved <i>Pay $7,999,999 To Access The Premium Version Of This Puzzle!</i> Guess we cheaped out on that one!</div>

## [The Desert Island](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/the_desert_island)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">This round was unlocked and had its feeders solved either overnight for me or while I was looking at the Atoll meta in the morning, although I did experience the "what the fuck" moment of opening it for the first time as I woke up, and it was the greatest amount of dread the initial page of a round had ever inflicted on me (in a good way)!</div>

<div style="margin-left: 30px;"><h3>[META] The Desert Island</h3></div>

<div style="margin-left: 60px;">So now all that was left was this meta, and we'd be on to the endgame! This one was a bit difficult to get working for us, we ended up losing some time trying several things. One of our ideas included interpreting some of the answers as multiplication, especially GROSS, but also HUNDRED TWO as ×100 +2, like if you were reading the numbers concatenated with these phrases, so for example 3 GROSS would be 432 and 3 HUNDRED TWO would be 302. Other theories included: extrapolating what pieces outside of the 1–10000 range would be; trying to look at pieces from the transformed numbers and their puzzle contents, rather than just the numbers; trying to do a calculation with the three numbers together; looking at the differences between any used pieces to see if we could spot the answer numbers there; etc. I think we were always hovering around the right thing to do, but never quite got there. We just needed the hint of "Apply the feeder answers to the piece numbers that contributed to them." which is something we had already tried briefly but for some reason didn't make clear on our hint request, to get us going. After we got the new numbers with the correct transformations of only adding or subtracting, me and Autumn both thought of morse independently, which was probably a good sign that was the right thing to do, but I kept getting gibberish, I was trying to read differences of 1 as dots, and differences of 2 as dashes, but turns out differences of 1 mean a sequence of numbers that can be grouped together to make a dash, as ffao correctly interpreted. It took me a while to get past the mental rigidity of interpreting the puzzle only in the way I first thought to, but I think this does make plenty more sense than how we were originally trying. This meta also feels a bit bad that we had to hint because we were dancing so close to the right thing all this time but just never got to it, but I guess that's the problem with a mostly number-based meta, that it's not very easy to immediately discern any correct or incorrect interpretations because numbers don't spell gibberish like a wrongly extracted string of letters do! I also ended up thinking this was probably the weakest implementation of the pieces gimmick, this round could've been ported to a non-piece-based hunt very easily, and I do think it would've been cool to have meta relevant info on the unused pieces (although I guess that has been done before), but still, the idea here is really cool, and in the end I'm very much glad this round was included anyway!</div>

## [Your Very Own Island](https://2025.galacticpuzzlehunt.com/round/your_island)

<div style="margin-left: 30px;">And now it was time for the endgame! I was a bit surprised to see only one puzzle here from realizing we were missing six puzzles by the leaderboard count, but better focus on what's available at hand here now, right? This is a very rule-dense logic puzzle to act as the capstone of the hunt. Work here was slow, I think everyone was a bit too tired (I certainly was), and it was a bit tricky to keep all the rules in mind while solving this (again, at least for my tired brain), but we eventually pushed through until the final step that had us having to "vibe out" the last few pieces. After some staring, I got the Australia aha, and it was a pretty fun moment! We'd solved GPH 2025! Despite getting the final aha, this capstone still didn't quite feel super satisfying to me, maybe it's because I still haven't fully came around to non-answerables, maybe it was the quantity of rules to make everything work, maybe a bit of both, I'm not sure! But it was still a neat way to round out the hunt, and it took me until the final cutscene to realize the Laura + Sia and Pan + Gaia puns...</div><br>

Another cool thing is we got access to all the solutions too from the prior puzzles we'd solved, which I thought was a really cool addition to this hunt, that I hope more hunts copy, especially as the huge fan of reading author's notes that I am! And we also unlocked some extra puzzles! Scratch everything I've said about The Atoll, [META] The Ultimate Puzzle is my new favorite meta now!

So all in all, this was a fantastic hunt, that completely consumed my free time for nearly 6 full days, but I wouldn't have had it any other day. It was exactly the kind of hard but rewarding hunt I was looking forward to after coming back from my puzzle burnout, and so many things on this hunt were amazing, some brilliant metas, clever feeders, a very unique gimmick that was well-utilized throughout most of the rounds! I also really value challenging puzzles and hunts as a whole. They make the ahas you get feel so much more powerful, because you know how long you were stuck for until you got them, you know how unrelenting this hunt has been so far, so they just hit extra hard, and that's a wonderful feeling! It reminds me a bit of the concept of tension and release in music theory, getting an aha in a beginner-friendly hunt is more like a plagal cadence, you can feel the resolution, but it's not very strong, contrast that with a V7♭9 chord resolving to the I, and it just pushes you with so much more force, even if you do have to withstand more dissonance to get there! I hope that makes sense, I really like this analogy! Of course, no hunt is perfect, though, I don't think it'd be fair to omit the fact that the low width did keep us stuck, for over 24h no less, but this hunt was still one of my all-time favorites, and one that I'll cherish for a long time to come!

I felt like I'd made a lot more contributions towards the puzzles than I'd done in the previous years too, before my puzzle burnout, and I really could feel my improvement as a solver, even if there are long ways to go still. I don't know exactly how this improvement could've happened, since I didn't exactly practice in the meantime, but I just think having my enthusiasm and energy revitalized helped me push through the hard puzzles a lot more than I'd have done a few years ago, and that really makes me thrilled as an indication that the puzzle burnout has been completely defeated for the time being! Now I'm thrilled awaiting for next year's Mystery Hunt already, which is a feeling I don't think I've had with that same force since 2021's. It was also a pleasure solving alongside my friends on Blob Wizards. I'd solved with the group before, but I joined them as the burnout was kicking in, and many times I'd end up not contributing much or at all to the hunts I'd signed up with them (although I'd always warn them in advance that this could happen and that I didn't want to take away a slot of anyone that would more consistently contribute), so this felt like the first time I got to solve a really meaty hunt with them to my fullest, with it really bringing out teamwork to the limelight with the scale and difficulty involved! All things considered, this hunt has done an immense amount of good for me, and I'm grateful for everyone on 🛫🛫🛫 Galactic Trendsetters 🛬🛬🛬 for making this happen, as well as my Blob Wizards teammates for taking me on for this year's edition!
